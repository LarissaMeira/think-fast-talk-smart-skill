# 🎯 Think Fast Talk Smart (Claude Skill)

**A Claude skill that coaches you on how you communicated — grounded in the actual conversation you just had.**

Based on the frameworks of Matt Abrahams, professor of Strategic Communication at Stanford GSB.

---

## The source

I built this skill from the transcript of the 300th episode of Matt Abrahams' podcast. If you only watch one video on communication this year, make it this one — every framework inside the skill traces back to it.

## The problem it solves

You already know what good communication looks like. You listened to the podcast, read the book, saved the frameworks. You can name PREP, BLUF, active listening.

And then you walk into a meeting and default to the same patterns: bury the point, over-explain, propose before you paraphrase.

The gap is not knowledge. It is feedback. Nobody sits with you after a meeting and says "here is exactly where you lost the room." Without that, every meeting is a missed rep.

This skill closes that gap. It reads what you actually said, diagnoses the specific moment where the message broke, and tells you what to try in the next conversation.

---

## What it does

Think Fast Talk Smart activates in two moments:

**Before a meeting** — you tell Claude who you're meeting with and what you need to land. The skill builds your message using BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front), structures the body with PREP or What / So What / Now What, translates any technical jargon into executive language, and gives you an anxiety-management protocol for the 10 seconds before you begin.

**After a meeting** — you paste the transcript or describe how it went. The skill diagnoses whether your main point came first or got buried, flags jargon that created distance, checks whether you paraphrased before proposing, and rewrites the sharpest moments the way they should have sounded. Every analysis ends with one concrete adjustment for the next conversation.

---

## The frameworks inside

- **PREP** (Point, Reason, Example, Point) — to defend a position or present an idea
- **What? So What? Now What?** — for updates, feedback, and introductions
- **BLUF** — because executives read titles, not slides
- **Lingo vs. Jargon** — a translation guide from technical language into business language
- **The ABC method** (Affect, Behavior, Cognition) — to manage anxiety before high-stakes meetings
- **Active paraphrasing** — because whoever proposes first sounds like a vendor; whoever paraphrases first sounds like a strategic partner

## Where it fits in my system

I use it inside my **Sherlock Meetings** Claude project. After every important meeting, the transcript goes through the Meeting Insights Tracker skill (structured record in Notion) and then through Think Fast Talk Smart (coaching on how I communicated). Each meeting is an isolated diagnosis — no mixing points from different conversations.

The result: I don't repeat the same communication mistakes across weeks of meetings. Each conversation becomes a data point in a personal improvement loop.

## How to use it

1. Download `SKILL.md` from this repo.
2. Add it to your Claude account under Skills, or drop it into a `.claude/skills/think-fast-talk-smart/` folder in your project.
3. Trigger it with phrases like:
   - *"Help me prepare what to say in the meeting with [person]."*
   - *"Review how I communicated in this transcript."*
   - *"I want to sound more strategic in front of leadership."*
4. For post-meeting analysis, paste the transcript or your notes. The skill takes it from there.

## Make it yours

The examples inside the skill are drawn from my world: AI consulting, executive meetings, technical translation. If your work looks different, do not rewrite the skill by hand. Save it to your Claude account, open a fresh chat, and paste:

> *"Rewrite all the examples in the Think Fast Talk Smart skill using my professional context: [describe your work in one sentence]. Keep every framework and structure identical, only change the examples."*

Claude will produce a version tailorado to your reality: sales, medicine, engineering, law, whatever you do. Same coach, your language.

## Why I built it

I work at the intersection of business and technology. The vocabulary keeps shifting, but the executive room does not. Decision-makers still want the point in the first sentence. They still tune out on jargon. They still trust the person who listens before proposing.

Honestly, I built this because consuming content is not enough. You can watch every podcast, read every book, save every framework, and none of it changes how you show up in the next meeting unless you build a way to put it into practice.

This skill is my practice loop. It takes what I learned from Matt Abrahams and turns it into feedback on my own conversations, one meeting at a time.
